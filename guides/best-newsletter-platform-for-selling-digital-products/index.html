Which newsletter platform is cheapest once you actually sell something?

Every platform on this page will happily take a payment for you. What separates them is the fee, the plan you must be on before checkout unlocks, and how many products you are allowed to list. Below is the arithmetic, worked out at two realistic revenue levels.

Selling under $2,000/month

Fees barely matter. Pick on workflow: Kit if you want commerce on a free plan, Systeme.io if you want funnels and a course platform too.

Selling more than that

Percentage fees start to dominate. Systeme.io and beehiiv take 0%; Substack's 10% becomes the most expensive line you have.

The fees, stated plainly

Two different things get called "fees" and they are not comparable. Some platforms quote a rate that already includes card processing; others take their cut and then hand you to Stripe, which takes another. The table separates them.

Two things fall out of that table. First, the gap between Kit and a 0%-platform tool is roughly 0.6% — about $6 a month at $1,000 in sales and $60 at $10,000. That is a real number but it is not a reason to migrate on its own. Second, Substack is in a different category: at $10,000 a month it costs roughly $1,000 more than everything else on this page, every month.

Where each one actually fits

Kit

The only platform here that gives you a checkout on a free plan alongside a 10,000-subscriber list. If you have not proven the product yet, that combination is hard to beat: you pay nothing until something sells, and then you pay a percentage of money you have already received.

Not for you if selling is the main event and volume is high — at that point the percentage is worth engineering around.

Systeme.io

0% transaction fees, plus sales funnels, a course platform and an affiliate programme on the free plan at 2,000 contacts. If your product is a course rather than a file, this replaces two or three tools at once.

Not for you if you care about email craft: the sending side is the weakest part of the bundle.

The mistake worth avoiding

People choose the platform with the lowest fee and then discover the checkout cannot do the thing they needed — a bundle, a coupon, an order bump, VAT handling for EU buyers, or simply more than five products. Fees are the easiest thing to compare, which is why they get compared, but the constraint that actually costs you is usually the plan cap.

Before you commit, write down: how many products, what price, which countries, and whether you need subscriptions or one-off payments. Then check that specific combination against the plan you intend to pay for, not the plan on the marketing page.

Bottom line

Selling occasionally to a list you already have: Kit's free plan, because the cost is zero until a sale happens. Building a course business: Systeme.io, because 0% fees plus funnels and course hosting on a free tier is a genuinely different offer. Selling subscriptions to a newsletter: beehiiv, once the list justifies $43/month. And if you are on Substack with real paid revenue, run the 10% against your own numbers — that is the single most expensive fee decision on this page.
