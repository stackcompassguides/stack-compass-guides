Kit vs MailerLite: which is better for creators?

Kit is the stronger pick if you plan to earn from an audience: commerce is built into every plan and the free plan carries 10,000 subscribers. MailerLite is the better call if cost and simplicity matter most — $12/month entry against Kit's $39/month.

Pick Kit

You want the list to become a business: products, paid newsletters, sponsorships, memberships.

Pick MailerLite

You want low-cost, straightforward campaigns and a drag-and-drop visual editor.

Start free on Kit

Kit's free Newsletter plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited broadcasts. Paid plans list a 14-day trial with no credit card required. Checked on Kit's pricing page, 18 August 2026.

Choose Kit if…

  • You plan to sell digital products, paid newsletters or memberships — Kit includes commerce on every plan, including the free one.
  • Your list will pass a few hundred people before you want to pay: the free plan runs to 10,000 subscribers.
  • You want to organise people by tags and behaviour rather than by separate lists.
  • Newsletter growth features matter to you — Kit documents Recommendations and newsletter sponsorships.

Choose MailerLite if…

  • The cheapest paid plan wins: Comfort starts at $12/month, Kit's Creator plan at $39/month billed monthly.
  • You want a drag-and-drop visual editor with templates and 70+ content blocks.
  • You have a small list but need more than one automation — the free plan includes 3.
  • You want a website builder alongside landing pages and forms in one tool.

Where each one is weaker

Kit's downsides

  • The paid entry point is roughly three times MailerLite's: $39/month against $12/month.
  • The free plan allows one basic automation, so any real automation work pushes you onto a paid plan.
  • Kit's own documentation notes that the free plan carries a Kit-managed recommendation slot, with those earnings going to Kit.

MailerLite's downsides

  • 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month is a small free tier; an audience that grows will hit it quickly.
  • Comfort caps sending at 10× your subscriber tier and automations at 50.
  • Digital products are capped at 5 on Comfort, so a growing product catalogue means the Power plan.

Our recommendation

For a creator who intends to build products, paid newsletters, memberships or sponsorships around an email audience, we would start with Kit. The reason is not a feature checklist — it is that Kit's free plan lets an audience grow to 10,000 subscribers with commerce available the whole way, so the platform does not force a paid decision before there is revenue to justify it.

For someone who mainly needs affordable, well-designed newsletters and campaigns, we would lean toward MailerLite. Once you are paying either way, it is the cheaper platform, and it stays cheaper as the list grows.

If both descriptions sound partly true, the practical test is whether you expect money to flow from the list within the next year. If yes, Kit. If no, MailerLite.

Who should not start with Kit

  • You send an occasional newsletter and have no plan to monetize it. The extra creator tooling will not be used.
  • You are optimizing purely for cost. MailerLite is cheaper on every paid tier we compared.
  • You need several automations right away but do not want to pay yet. MailerLite's free plan allows three; Kit's allows one.
  • You need a full CRM or sales pipeline rather than creator publishing. Neither tool is the right category.

Decide in 60 seconds

  1. Will this list sell something within a year? Yes → Kit. No → MailerLite.
  2. How big will the list get before you are willing to pay? Past 250 → Kit's free plan buys you a lot more time.
  3. Is a low monthly bill the deciding factor? Yes → MailerLite, at every paid tier.

Bottom line

Both tools send email reliably and both have a free plan you can start on today without a credit card. The split is about what happens next: Kit is built for turning an audience into income and gives you a long free runway to get there, MailerLite is built to keep email cheap and simple.
