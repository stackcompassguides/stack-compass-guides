What creator email free plans actually include

"Free plan" means six different things across these six platforms. One gives you 10,000 subscribers and almost no automation. One gives you unlimited subscribers and takes a cut of your revenue instead. The useful question is not how generous the free tier looks — it is which wall you will hit first.

The three numbers that matter

1. The subscriber cap decides how long free lasts

MailerLite's 250 is a trial in everything but name; a decent lead magnet blows through it in weeks. Kit's 10,000 is a genuinely long runway — for most newsletters, years. beehiiv, EmailOctopus and Systeme.io sit in between at 2,000–2,500, which is roughly a year of steady growth.

2. The sending limit decides how often you can write

A cap on subscribers is not the same as a cap on emails. MailerLite's free plan allows 2,500 emails a month, so at 250 subscribers you can send ten times a month — fine for a weekly. EmailOctopus allows 10,000 emails against 2,500 subscribers: four sends a month, so a weekly newsletter fits exactly and a twice-weekly does not.

3. The automation limit decides whether the list works while you sleep

This is where free plans are quietly restrictive. beehiiv's free plan has no automations at all, so a welcome email — the single highest-performing email any newsletter sends — requires the $43/month plan. Kit allows one basic automation, which covers exactly that welcome sequence and nothing else. MailerLite's three automations are, in practice, the most generous free automation allowance here.

Which free plan for which situation

Starting from zero, no product yet

Kit. 10,000 subscribers and a checkout on the free plan means you can grow and even sell without paying anything. The one-automation limit is the price you pay, and early on you only need one.

You want automations before you want scale

MailerLite. Three automations on free is the best allowance here — as long as you stay under 250 subscribers, which you will not for long.

Publication first, with a real website

beehiiv. The free plan includes a full site, archive and custom domain, which none of the others give away. Just do not expect to automate anything.

Selling a course from day one

Systeme.io. Funnels, a course, unlimited sending and 0% transaction fees at 2,000 contacts is the most product-shaped free plan on the list.

What "free" costs you anyway

  • Branding. EmailOctopus puts its name at the bottom of your emails until you pay.
  • Recommendation slots. Kit's free plan carries a Kit-managed recommendation, and the revenue from it goes to Kit.
  • Migration debt. Every subscriber you add on the wrong platform is one you will have to move later. Free plans are cheapest at the start and most expensive at the point you outgrow them.
  • Deliverability you cannot see. Free tiers usually share sending infrastructure with everyone else on the free tier. It rarely matters at small volumes, and it is not nothing.
