Kit vs beehiiv: which is better for creators?

These two are not competing on features so much as on business models. Kit assumes your list is an audience you will sell things to. beehiiv assumes the newsletter itself is the product, and gives you ads, paid subscriptions and referral growth to monetize it.

Pick Kit

You sell courses, downloads, coaching or memberships, and email is how you sell them.

Pick beehiiv

The newsletter is the business: sponsorships, an ad network, paid subscriptions, referrals.

Start free on Kit

Kit's free plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers. beehiiv's free plan stops at 2,500 — but includes a website and custom domain. Checked on both pricing pages, 19 August 2026.

Choose Kit if…

  • Your revenue comes from products you sell to the list, not from the list itself.
  • You want a long free runway: 10,000 subscribers free, against beehiiv's 2,500.
  • You need real automation logic — tagging, behaviour triggers, branching sequences.
  • You are migrating from Mailchimp or MailerLite and think in terms of funnels and tags.

Choose beehiiv if…

  • You want sponsorship revenue without selling ads yourself — beehiiv's Ad Network matches sponsors to newsletters.
  • You plan to charge for the newsletter: paid subscriptions carry a 0% platform take rate on paid plans.
  • Your list will pass 10,000 subscribers, where Kit's per-subscriber pricing starts to bite.
  • You want the newsletter, the website and the archive to be one product with a custom domain, on the free plan.

Price at a glance

The two price on different axes, which is what makes the comparison awkward. Kit prices per subscriber from the first paid tier. beehiiv prices in flat bands that run to 100,000 subscribers. Figures below were read from both pricing pages on 19 August 2026.

Kit

  • Newsletter (free): up to 10,000 subscribers, unlimited broadcasts, 1 basic visual automation.
  • Creator: $39/month billed monthly, or $390/year at 1,000 subscribers.
  • Pro: $790/year at 1,000 subscribers, adds subscriber scoring and engagement analytics.
  • Selling: 3.5% + 30¢ per transaction, on every plan.

beehiiv

  • Launch (free): up to 2,500 subscribers, unlimited sends, website, custom domain, 3 publications. No automations, no paid subscriptions, no Ad Network.
  • Scale: $43/month, or $517/year — up to 100,000 subscribers, Ad Network, automations, paid subscriptions, digital products.
  • Max: $96/month, or $1,151/year — 10 publications, white-label, Send API.
  • Paid subscriptions: 0% take rate on paid plans (Stripe fees still apply).

Where the money actually lands: below about 2,500 subscribers both are free, so price is not the deciding factor. Between 2,500 and 10,000 Kit is free and beehiiv costs $43/month. Above roughly 10,000 that reverses hard — beehiiv stays at $43/month all the way to 100,000 subscribers, while Kit's price climbs with the list.

Where each one is weaker

Kit's downsides

  • Per-subscriber pricing gets expensive on a large list, which is exactly the point where a newsletter business becomes viable.
  • 3.5% + 30¢ on every sale is not a small fee once you are selling seriously.
  • No equivalent of an ad network — sponsorship revenue is more of a manual sales job.
  • The free plan carries a Kit-managed recommendation slot, and those earnings go to Kit.

beehiiv's downsides

  • The free plan has no automations at all, so even a welcome sequence means $43/month.
  • 2,500 free subscribers is a short runway compared to Kit's 10,000.
  • Automation logic is shallower than Kit's if you run genuine behavioural funnels.
  • The monetization features that justify the price only pay off at a size most newsletters never reach.

Our recommendation

If you already sell something — a course, templates, coaching, a membership — start with Kit. The automation depth is the part you will actually use, and the free plan means you do not pay until the list is large enough to matter.

If the plan is to build a readership and monetize the readership, beehiiv is the better-shaped tool, and the flat pricing above 10,000 subscribers is a real structural advantage. The catch is that the useful monetization features are all behind the $43/month plan, so beehiiv asks you to pay before you have revenue, while Kit does not.

A practical middle path: start free on Kit while the list is small and you are still figuring out what you sell. Moving a list is annoying but it is not hard, and it is cheaper than paying $43/month for two years to find out the newsletter is not the business.

Who should not pick either

  • You want the simplest possible cheap newsletter and will never sell anything. MailerLite or EmailOctopus will cost you less.
  • You want zero platform cost and are willing to give up a percentage instead — that is Substack's model, covered in the digital products guide.
  • You need a full CRM with deals and pipelines. Neither of these is that category.

Decide in 60 seconds

  1. Does the money come from the newsletter, or through it? From it → beehiiv. Through it → Kit.
  2. Will the list pass 10,000 in the next year? Yes → beehiiv's flat band saves real money. No → Kit is free for longer.
  3. Do you need automations before you have revenue? Yes → Kit, because beehiiv has none on free.
